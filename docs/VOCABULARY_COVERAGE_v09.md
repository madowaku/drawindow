## Normalization and names

The audit invokes words.ts directly. EN lowercases and trims, strips edge punctuation, folds whitespace, then accepts Latin words with spaces, apostrophes or hyphens. Both languages reject digits, angle/curly/square brackets, slash/backslash, inputs over 80 characters and normalized words over 64 characters. JA first resolves an unambiguous naming-dictionary form, otherwise NFKC-normalizes, trims allowed edge punctuation and converts katakana to hiragana; only kana and ー remain valid. Small kana expand only for first/last chain units, and terminal ー resolves to the preceding vowel. The visible word is retained. Chain comparison additionally folds voiced/semi-voiced kana.

JA maps only 177 model categories; 168 abstain. Top-1 remains authoritative: no lower-ranked category is selected to repair a chain. Alternatives can retain unmapped English strings and do not expand the accepted top-1 vocabulary. Equal normalized readings do not prove equal meanings. Model collisions: くも = cloud / spider; くるま = car / van; はな = flower / nose. Naming aliases such as 犬/いぬ share a reading; わんこ keeps its own reading. 梨/洋梨/ラ・フランス remain なし/ようなし/らふらんす. とり/すずめ and はな/ひまわり remain distinct. The dictionary's 花 and 鼻 are ambiguous はな readings, not a semantic synonym claim. Full dictionary and IDs of templates sharing normalized names are enumerated in JSON.

## Missing and sparse starting sounds

Missing means no selectable nonterminal reply (JA excludes drawings ending ん as gameReducer does). Sparse means one or two distinct playable words with matching starting sound, including voiced relaxation. EN checks a-z; JA checks the modern unvoiced kana plus を and ん. Observed voiced forms remain separately visible in the distributions.

EN missing: none. Sparse: j (2), q (2), x (1), y (2), z (2).

JA missing: を, ん. Sparse: つ (2), ぬ (1), の (2), み (2), め (1), よ (2), る (1), わ (2).

## Deterministic chains and uncovered_letter

Seeds 1-256 use a fixed LCG (1664525, 1013904223, unsigned 32-bit). Each synthetic player chooses uniformly among distinct normalized model words whose first sound matches Shizuku's current ending. Selectors are the imported production functions, with four recognition transitions and the fifth player turn unrecognized, matching DONE. JA uses real starter rotation, terminal-ん restart, nonterminal reply filtering, used IDs, mode heuristic and rotating slip slot; a slip is corrected before the next player turn. Seed is the sessionId, starter index is seed-1. This is a finite-vocabulary stress sample, not a player behavior model.

An exhaustive real-gameReducer transition for every mapped model class reports EN 0/345, JA 0/177 uncovered errors; JA 17 terminal restarts. Exact uncovered model labels: EN none; JA none.

The real reducer reproduces uncovered_letter with JA ゐ → ゐ in the full production pool. EN covers every a-z starting letter, so a valid normalized EN word cannot reproduce that guard with the full pool; EN iraq → q reproduces it only in a deliberately reduced fixture with q templates removed. These are synthetic fault probes, not claims the local model emits them. The result remains recognition_error, with the drawing retained by the existing reducer. An arbitrary API word can exceed this model vocabulary. Coverage alone does not justify replacing the guess, adding hints, declaring recognition correct or marking a broken restart connected.

## Limits

No model weights were executed and no drawn strokes were recognized. The 0.05 default model score cutoff, actual top-1 distribution, recognition latency, human naming, blind readability, real-device comfort and fun are unmeasured. Uniform word sampling is intentionally unlike real model outputs. Catalogue name equality cannot establish that art depicts a model class, and homonyms can overstate semantic overlap. Source hashes make changes reviewable; rerun after catalogue/selection changes. No production words, art, model, selection policy or game rules are modified by this audit.
